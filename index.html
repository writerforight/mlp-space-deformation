Quick guide
  1. Look: the strip at the bottom is the network: Input ▸ W₁ | tanh ▸ … ▸ Output. Press ▶, click a step, or drag along the strip — objects and the grid go through every layer (linear step, then activation).
  2. Draw: ＋ Objects (bottom left) — pick a tool, draw on the view, or add a ready-made shape. Every object is resampled to Points points in total (Drawing settings), equally spaced along it.
  3. Inspect: ⓘ next to a layer in the strip — weights, activation and history. Double-click a weight to change it. ◫ shows the neuron diagram.
  4. Train: Model ▲ opens network, goal and training settings; pick a task, press ★ Recommended, then ▶ Train. With my own goals you mix goals (class → point, separate classes, object → point, object stays) and drag their targets at the output.
  5. More: View ▾ for overlays, Analysis for loss and diagnostics, Experiments for what a network cannot do.

How to read this chart

The linear step gives every point a number z = W a + b per neuron; the activation turns it into σ(z). The blue line is σ itself (input z → output). The teal area is its slope σ′(z): tall where σ is steep, near zero where σ is flat. The grey bars show where the points actually are on this layer's z axis (the grid, the objects and the data).

Points in the steep middle: σ is almost a straight line there, so the layer barely bends space. Points in the shaded flat parts: very different z values are squashed onto almost the same output (≈ ±1 for tanh). This is where space is folded — useful for classification, because a whole class can be pressed onto one value. The price: the slope is ≈ 0, so the gradient hardly passes and that neuron learns slowly (saturation). For ReLU everything below 0 becomes 0; pink regions mark where σ turns back (sin, GELU) and folds space onto itself.

Watch the bars while training: when they move from the middle into the flat parts, the network is bending space harder at this layer.
